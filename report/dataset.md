# Dataset

**Source.** Petrobras 3W Dataset 2.0.0, event 9 ("Hydrate in Production
Line"), CC BY 4.0. The event-9 folder contains 57 real instances, 150
simulated instances, and 594 Normal-operation instances (verified counts,
matching the dataset's own documentation exactly -- `DATA_FINDINGS.md` §1).

**Instance length.** Each 3W instance is a full-duration, 1 Hz multivariate
recording. Measured over the 57 real event-9 instances: **32,135 timesteps**
at the median (min 7,144, max 192,581); 55 of 57 exceed 10,000 timesteps
individually, and the 2 that fall short are still well above the floor in
aggregate reading (`DATA_FINDINGS.md` §1). Simulated instances run longer and
more uniformly (median 45,599, all 150 above 10,000); Normal-operation
instances have the widest spread (median 21,474, min 10,610, max 143,231).

**Label composition -- the positive class is rare.** Of the 57 real event-9
instances, only **14** ever contain a transient-phase label and only **3**
ever reach the established-blockage label; the remaining 43 contain only
normal and unlabeled observations. This motivates the project's
`failure_time` decision: lead time is measured against transient onset (14
events, 7 wells), not blockage onset (n=3, undefined in most cross-validation
folds) -- see `DATA_FINDINGS.md` §3 and §7 for the reasoning and the two
rejected alternatives. Figure [transient-duration] shows the distribution of
transient-phase durations across those 14 events (median 3.4 h, min 3 min, max
13.7 h) against the 30-minute window span the models see; Figure
[annotated-trace] shows one full instance
(`WELL-00042_20141218051903`) with its Normal / Transient / Established zones
shaded, where the pressure ramp in `P-JUS-CKGL` and `P-ANULAR` and the
temperature rise in `T-TPT` are visible well before the blockage is annotated.
Both are regenerated by `python -m tools.m1_dataset_figures --root
data/3W/dataset` into `figures/`.

**Label monotonicity.** `windowing.is_monotonic_severity()`, run over every
real event-9 instance, returns **True for 57/57** -- no instance's severity
ever reverts from Established back to Transient, or Transient back to Normal.
This is a decidable property of the data, not a modeling choice, and it
settles a design question for free: `label_window(rule="most_severe")` and
`label_window(rule="final_timestep")` are mathematically identical for every
window drawn from these instances (the max of a non-decreasing sequence is
its last element). The sweep below confirms this empirically as well.

**Unlabeled spans.** Every one of the 57 real instances carries unlabeled
(NaN) spans in the raw `class` column -- median 11.2% of samples, up to 50.4%
in the worst instance (`DATA_FINDINGS.md` §5). The project statement defines
no policy for these; we drop windows whose label span touches an unlabeled
sample (`nan_label_policy="drop"`, the conservative reading: an unlabeled span
is not evidence of normal operation), and the sweep below prices that choice
exactly.

**Sensor availability: missing and frozen are two different failures.**
`src/data/availability.py` measures both per variable over the 57 real
event-9 instances, length-weighted (full table:
`results/variable_availability.csv`). A frozen run -- at least 60 identical
consecutive 1 Hz readings -- is counted separately from a NaN, because a stuck
sensor is not a gap: it reports a plausible number that no imputation flags.
The two are disjoint by construction (a NaN breaks a run rather than extending
it), so they sum to an effective unavailability:

| Variable | % missing | % frozen | % effective | Kept |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `P-MON-CKP` | 0.0 | 1.1 | 1.1 | yes |
| `P-JUS-CKGL` | 0.0 | 2.3 | 2.3 | yes |
| `QGL` | 8.5 | 0.4 | 9.0 | yes |
| `T-TPT` | 26.1 | 10.1 | 36.2 | yes |
| `T-JUS-CKP` | 34.6 | 5.2 | 39.9 | yes |
| `P-ANULAR` | 43.1 | 1.2 | 44.3 | yes |
| **`P-TPT`** | **15.0** | **35.0** | **50.0** | **yes (by 0.003%)** |
| `ABER-CKP` | 34.9 | 59.6 | 94.6 | no |
| 19 further channels (`ESTADO-*`, `QBS`, `PT-P`, ...) | -- | -- | 100.0 | no |

Two observations matter for the rest of the paper. First, **`P-TPT` clears the
`max_missing_frac = 0.5` threshold by 0.00003** -- it is retained or discarded
essentially by rounding, and 70% of its unavailability is frozen rather than
missing. It is exactly the channel that `DATA_FINDINGS.md` §9 found dead
through three whole instances, one of them a blockage event. A threshold this
close to a coin flip is not a channel-selection method, which is why the
channel set below is chosen by event coverage instead. Second, the discrete
valve-state channels (`ESTADO-*`) are 100% unavailable, split between missing
and frozen -- they carry no information for this event in this release, and
their apparent presence in the schema is not presence in the data.

Note on scope: this table is measured over the 57 real event-9 instances,
while `build_cache`'s own channel fit spans all 801 instances (real +
simulated + Normal-operation), so the two sets of fractions are not directly
comparable -- the simulated instances have neither gaps nor frozen sensors.

## Well x channel trade-off

Different wells instrument different sensors, and some sensors are frozen
(stuck at a constant reading) for the entire duration of specific instances.
Selecting channels by *well* coverage measures the wrong thing: the criterion
that matters is *event* coverage, because three instances have `P-TPT`/`T-TPT`
frozen throughout, and one of those three is one of only three blockage events
in the whole real dataset (`DATA_FINDINGS.md` §8-9).

Measured on the built caches (not estimated -- these are the counts the models
actually train on):

| Channel set | Channels | Transient events | Blockage events | Positive wells | Real normal hours |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **`P-MON-CKP, P-JUS-CKGL, T-TPT, T-JUS-CKP, P-ANULAR`** (primary) | 5 | **14/14** | **3/3** | **7/7** | 1,937.7 h |
| `P-TPT, T-TPT` (sensitivity arm) | 2 | 10/14 | 2/3 | 5/7 | 2,885.7 h |

The primary 5-channel arm is used for every result in this paper. Its 1,937.7
normal hours are 19x the false-alarm calibration budget of 1 alarm per 100 h,
while an event lost to channel selection cannot be recovered downstream by any
model. The `P-TPT`/`T-TPT` arm buys 948 more normal hours -- it retains
WELL-00002, the single largest Normal-operation well -- at the cost of 4 of
14 transient events, 1 of 3 blockages, and 2 of 7 positive wells.

*Reconciliation note.* `DATA_FINDINGS.md` §9 estimated this trade-off at the
instance level (9/14 events, 4/7 wells, 1,467 h for the primary arm's
counterpart) using each instance's mean usable fraction. The built caches keep
somewhat more than that estimate predicted, because `min_valid_frac` is applied
**per window**, not per instance: an instance whose average usable fraction sits
below the threshold can still contain individual windows above it. The
direction of the conclusion is unchanged -- the estimator was conservative, not
wrong -- but the numbers reported here supersede it, since they describe the
actual cache.

## Sensitivity sweeps

Three one-factor-at-a-time sweeps around the primary config
(`tools/m1_sensitivity_sweep.py`; full table in
`results/m1_sensitivity_sweep.csv`). Window counts are dominated by the 150
simulated instances, so the columns that decide anything are the real-data
event counts:

| Arm | Windows kept | Windows dropped | Real transient events | Real blockage events | Real positive wells |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **primary** (`most_severe`, `drop`, 5 ch) | 86,088 | 48,882 | 14 | 3 | 7 |
| `label_rule=final_timestep` | 86,088 | 48,882 | 14 | 3 | 7 |
| `label_rule=majority` | 86,088 | 48,882 | **12** | **2** | **6** |
| `channels=P-TPT,T-TPT` | 98,315 | 36,655 | 10 | 2 | 5 |
| `nan_label_policy=normal` | 94,498 | 40,472 | 14 | 3 | 7 |

Three things follow, and two of them are worth a sentence in the paper:

1. **`most_severe` and `final_timestep` are identical in every column.** The
   monotonicity proof above is confirmed empirically on the real cache: the
   choice between the project's default rule and the standard alternative
   cannot change any result on this dataset.
2. **`majority` is not label-neutral -- it erases whole events.** It keeps the
   same number of windows but loses 2 of 14 transient events, 1 of 3
   blockages, and 1 positive well, because an event whose transient phase
   never occupies more than half of any 30-minute window is relabeled Normal
   everywhere and disappears from the positive class entirely. This is a
   concrete argument against majority labeling for rare, slow-onset events,
   not just a preference.
3. **The conservative NaN policy costs windows, but no events.** Counting
   unlabeled spans as Normal would add 8,410 windows (+9.8%), and event
   coverage is identical either way (14/3/7). The cost of the conservative
   choice is therefore purely statistical power, with no loss of positive
   examples -- which is what makes it cheap enough to prefer.
